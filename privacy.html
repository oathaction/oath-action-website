Privacy Policy

We built Oath & Action to earn trust, and that has to include how we treat your data. This policy is written in plain language on purpose — no buried clauses, no dark patterns, no surprises.

Oath & Action is an independent, nonpartisan civic-media nonprofit. This policy explains what information we collect when you read our work, subscribe to The Dispatch, or send us a message — and the choices you have. It applies to oathandaction.org and the email newsletters we send.

Our core promise: We never sell your data, and we don't take money to influence coverage. Our journalism is funded by readers and aligned philanthropy, never by advertisers buying access to you.

What we collect

We try to collect as little as possible. In practice, that comes down to three categories:

  • Information you give us. Your email address when you subscribe to The Dispatch; your name and message if you contact us or report a correction; and any details you submit through an action form, such as your ZIP code when we help route a message to your representative.
  • Information collected automatically. Privacy-respecting, aggregate usage data — which pages are popular, roughly where readers are (country/region, derived from a truncated IP address), and how the site performs. We do not build advertising profiles.
  • Information from email. If you open a newsletter, our delivery provider may record an aggregate open or click event so we can tell whether an issue landed. You can disable open tracking in most email clients, and we honor that.

How we use it

We use the information above only to do the things you'd expect from a newsroom:

  • To send you The Dispatch and any newsletters you've requested.
  • To respond to your messages, corrections, and tips.
  • To help you complete an action you started — for example, matching your ZIP code to the right legislative office.
  • To understand, in aggregate, which explainers and trackers are useful so we can make more of what helps.
  • To keep the site secure, fast, and free of abuse.

We do not use your data to target you with advertising, and we do not make automated decisions that produce legal or similarly significant effects about you.

Newsletter & analytics

The Dispatch is delivered through a third-party email service provider that acts as our processor under contract. They store your email address and engagement events solely to deliver our mail; they are not permitted to use your data for their own purposes.

For site analytics, we use a privacy-first product that does not set cross-site cookies, does not collect personally identifying information, and truncates IP addresses before storage. Reports we see are aggregate — counts and trends, not individual reading histories. We do not load Google Analytics, advertising pixels, or social-media tracking widgets.

Cookies

We use a small number of first-party cookies and similar local storage, limited to what keeps the site working:

  • Essential. Remembering that you dismissed a banner, or that you prefer reduced motion. These don't track you across sites.
  • Aggregate analytics. Our analytics tool is cookieless by default; where any identifier is used it is a rotating, non-personal value that cannot follow you to other websites.

We set no third-party advertising cookies. You can clear or block cookies in your browser at any time; the only thing you'll lose is a few small conveniences.

Data sharing & our no-sale pledge

We share personal data only with the service providers that help us operate — our email delivery service, our hosting and security providers, and our analytics tool — each bound by contract to use it solely on our behalf. We may also disclose information if required by a valid legal process, or to protect the rights and safety of our readers and staff.

We never sell your data; we don't take money to influence coverage. We do not rent, trade, or sell email lists. We do not accept payment to publish, suppress, or angle a story. Our independence is the product.

Your rights

Wherever you live, we extend the same core rights to everyone:

  • Access. Ask us what personal data we hold about you, and we'll tell you.
  • Export. Request a copy of your data in a portable, machine-readable format.
  • Delete. Ask us to erase your data, and we will — subject to narrow legal retention needs.
  • Correct. Fix anything inaccurate.
  • Unsubscribe. Every newsletter has a one-click unsubscribe; you don't need to ask us.

To exercise any of these, email privacy@oathandaction.org or use our contact form. We verify requests to protect your account, and we aim to respond within 30 days.

Children's privacy

Oath & Action is intended for a general audience and is not directed to children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal data from children under 13. If you believe a child has provided us information, contact us and we will delete it.

Security

We protect your data with industry-standard measures: encryption in transit (HTTPS everywhere), access controls that limit who on our small team can see subscriber data, and vendors selected in part for their security posture. No system is perfectly secure, but we treat your trust as the asset it is — and if a breach ever affected your data, we would notify you promptly and plainly.

Changes to this policy

If we make a material change to this policy, we'll update the date at the top and, for significant changes, note it in The Dispatch so subscribers hear about it directly. We won't quietly expand how we use your data and bury it in a revision.

Contact

Questions about privacy, or want to make a data request? Reach our team at privacy@oathandaction.org, or use the contact page. We read every message.
