Who we are

We're building the instrument panel for self-government.

Oath & Action is nonpartisan civic media and a democracy-reform platform. We explain how power actually works, track who is moving it across all 50 states, and hand people the next clear step. We answer to our readers — not a party, not a donor, not an algorithm.

Our mission

From spectators to stewards

Most Americans aren't apathetic. They're overwhelmed. The machinery of government has grown so opaque that even careful, civic-minded people can't tell where a decision gets made, who made it, or how to reach the lever in time to matter. That confusion isn't an accident — it's the natural state of a complicated system that no one is paid to make legible.

We exist to make it legible. We treat civic understanding the way a flight deck treats altitude and fuel: as something you should be able to read at a glance, trust completely, and act on immediately. Clarity, for us, isn't a nice-to-have. It's the precondition for a republic where ordinary people can actually steer.

A spectator watches the score. A steward tends the thing they've been entrusted with. Our entire product — every explainer, every tracker, every action — is designed to move one more person across that line.

A democracy doesn't run low on opinions. It runs low on people who can see the whole machine and know where to push.

The hard part

How we stay nonpartisan

Nonpartisan isn't a slogan you print on a masthead. It's a set of structures you build so that no single funder, faction, or mood can capture the work. Here's how we hold the line — and how you can check us.

Editorial independence

Our newsroom reports to an editor-in-chief, not to our development team. Funders see no story before publication, suggest no coverage, and hold no veto. A standing firewall policy — published in full — separates the people who raise money from the people who decide what we cover.

Transparent funding

Every grant over $5,000 is listed by name in our public ledger, updated quarterly. We take no money from political parties, candidate committees, PACs, or corporate lobbying arms — and we cap any single donor at 12% of annual revenue so no one can buy a tilt.

Methodology you can check

Our trackers cite their sources — bill numbers, vote records, filing IDs — and link straight to the primary documents. When we score momentum or label a reform "stalled," we publish the rubric. Disagree with a call? The receipts are one click away.

What guides the work

Our principles

Six commitments we re-read before we ship anything. They settle the hard calls when deadlines and incentives pull the other way.

01

Clarity is a duty

If a reader can't act on what we publish, we haven't finished the job. We write for the curious non-expert, every time.

02

Show the receipts

Every claim links to a source. Every score shows its math. Trust is earned in primary documents, not adjectives.

03

No tribe, no spin

We cover the system, not the scoreboard. We'll praise and criticize the same actor in the same month if the facts say so.

04

Process over outrage

Anger is cheap and exhausting. We trade it for understanding and a scoped next step that actually moves the needle.

05

Independence above growth

We'll turn down money and traffic before we'll take a string attached. The mission outlasts any quarter.

06

Built to be checked

We publish our methods, corrections, and funding so anyone can audit us. Accountability runs both ways.

Follow the money

How we're funded

We're a nonprofit newsroom funded by the people we serve and a vetted set of nonpartisan foundations — and nobody else.

Where our money comes from — and where it doesn't
Small-dollar members
About 64% of revenue comes from individual stewards giving $5–$25 a month. They're our largest and most important funder.
Nonpartisan foundations
Mission-aligned grants for civic education and open-data infrastructure. Every grant over $5,000 is named in our public ledger.
Single-donor cap
No funder may exceed 12% of annual revenue. No strings, no pre-publication review, no coverage requests — ever.
What we never take
No money from political parties, candidate committees, PACs, corporate lobbying arms, or anonymous dark-money vehicles.
