How we work

Editorial standards & ethics

Trust is not a tagline; it's a set of rules you can read, check, and hold us to. These are ours — built around three commitments: be nonpartisan by design, be accurate and correct ourselves in the open, and be transparent about who funds us and how we work.

Our principles

Six commitments we hold ourselves to

Each of these is a standard with teeth — something a reader could point to if we fell short.

01

Nonpartisan by design

We don't endorse candidates or parties. We explain how power works and let the mechanism, not the team jersey, drive the story.

02

Accuracy & corrections

We get it right before we publish, and when we don't, we fix it visibly and quickly. A correction is a feature, not an embarrassment.

03

Sourcing & transparency

We link to primary sources — bill text, filings, votes — so you can check our work without taking our word for it.

04

Independence from funders

No funder reviews coverage before publication. We refuse corporate, PAC, and pay-to-play money. Period.

05

Methodology you can check

Our trackers and data work come with published methods. If you can't reproduce it, we haven't finished explaining it.

06

Fact and analysis, labeled

Reporting, explainer, and analysis are clearly tagged. You always know whether you're reading a fact or a judgment.

Our nonpartisan commitment

Oath & Action does not endorse candidates, parties, or ballot positions. Our beat is the machinery of democracy itself — redistricting, voting access, money in politics, the rules that decide who holds power — and we cover reforms on their merits and mechanics, not on which side proposes them.

In practice this means: we name sponsors of both parties when a bill has bipartisan support; we describe what a reform does before we describe who likes it; and we apply the same scrutiny to a measure regardless of who introduced it. Staff are barred from public partisan activity — yard signs, donations, or campaign volunteering — for the offices and issues they cover.

Nonpartisan does not mean neutral about everything. We are openly pro-transparency, pro-accuracy, and pro-participation. We are simply not on anyone's team.

Sourcing & fact-checking

Every factual claim we publish is traceable to a source a reader could find. Wherever possible we link directly to the primary record — the bill text, the committee calendar, the disclosure filing, the recorded vote — rather than to coverage of it.

  • Primary first. We prefer the original document over a secondhand account of it.
  • Two-source rule. Contested factual claims are confirmed by at least two independent sources before publication.
  • Named over anonymous. We use on-the-record sources by default and grant anonymity only for a documented reason, never for convenience.
  • Pre-publication checks. Explainers and data pieces are reviewed by a second editor against their sources before they go live.

Corrections policy

When we're wrong, we say so. We correct the record promptly, mark the change clearly at the foot of the affected piece, and never quietly edit an error out of existence. Significant corrections are logged publicly (see the recent corrections below).

How to request a correction: email corrections@oathandaction.org or use the contact form with the article title, the specific claim you believe is wrong, and a source if you have one. A standards editor reviews every request, usually within two business days, and replies whether or not we make a change.

What counts as a correction vs. an update: A correction fixes a factual error and is always disclosed. An update adds new developments to an evolving story and is timestamped. We don't blur the two to make a mistake look like news.

Funding & conflicts of interest

Oath & Action is funded by reader contributions and grants from philanthropic foundations that support nonpartisan civic information. No funder — reader, foundation, or institutional partner — reviews, approves, or sees coverage before it publishes. Grants are accepted only without editorial conditions, and we disclose any funder with a stake in a story we cover.

We refuse advertising, corporate sponsorship tied to coverage, PAC money, and any pay-to-play arrangement. Staff recuse themselves from stories where they have a personal or financial conflict, and those recusals are noted to editors.

Use of data & methodology

Our trackers and data journalism ship with documented methods: where the data came from, how we cleaned and categorized it, what we counted, and what we deliberately left out. When we assign a status like "stalled" or "enacted," the criteria are defined and applied consistently across states.

We publish our error bars. If a number is an estimate, we say so; if a dataset is incomplete, we flag the gap rather than paper over it. Where licensing allows, we make underlying data available so others can reproduce or challenge our findings.

AI & automation disclosure

We use software tools, including AI, the way a newsroom uses any instrument: to work faster, never to replace human judgment or accountability. Concretely:

  • AI may help us draft summaries, transcribe public hearings, surface patterns in large datasets, or check our own copy for clarity.
  • A human editor is responsible for every published word. We do not publish unreviewed machine-generated text as journalism.
  • We do not use AI to fabricate quotes, images, or sources, and we label any synthetic illustration as such.
  • We do not feed reader data or confidential source material into third-party tools that could retain it.

Reader feedback

This page is a living document, and readers help keep us honest. If you think we've fallen short of a standard here — missed a source, blurred fact and opinion, or let a conflict slip — tell us. Email standards@oathandaction.org. We'd rather hear it from you than not hear it at all.

Independence

How we stay independent

The clearest way to judge a newsroom's independence is to ask who pays it — and what it refuses.

Funding & what we refuse
Where our money comes from
Reader memberships and small donations; multi-year grants from nonpartisan civic and journalism foundations, accepted without editorial conditions.
What we refuse
Corporate sponsorship tied to coverage, PAC and party money, advertising, and any pay-to-play or "sponsored coverage" arrangement.
Funder access to coverage
None. No funder sees, reviews, or approves a story before publication. Conflicts are disclosed in the piece.
Transparency
We publish an annual list of funders giving above $5,000 and an annual financial summary.
