Why this bill matters

One vote that decides a decade of maps

Redistricting happens once every ten years, but it sets the boundaries of nearly every state and congressional race in between. HB 1042 would hand that pen to an independent commission instead of the legislature drawing its own districts.

What it does

Reading the bill in plain language

HB 1042 takes the job of drawing state legislative and congressional districts away from the legislature and gives it to an independent, 13-member citizen commission. Commissioners are screened for conflicts of interest, drawn from a public applicant pool, and barred from having held partisan office or worked as a lobbyist in the preceding four years.

The commission would draw maps in open meetings, publish draft maps for public comment, and adopt final maps only with a supermajority that must include members not affiliated with the largest party. Courts would review challenges against fixed, neutral criteria — compactness, contiguity, and respect for county and community boundaries.

The core change: the people who run for office would no longer draw the lines that decide their own races. That single shift is what supporters and opponents are actually arguing about.

Key provisions in the current version:

  • Establishes a 13-member citizen commission with strict conflict-of-interest screening.
  • Requires open meetings, published draft maps, and a public-comment period before adoption.
  • Sets neutral, ranked map criteria and bars drawing maps to favor a party or incumbent.
  • Requires a cross-affiliation supermajority to adopt final maps.
  • Provides for expedited, criteria-based judicial review of any challenge.
